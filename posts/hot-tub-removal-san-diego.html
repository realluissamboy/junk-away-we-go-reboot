Hot tub removal in San Diego: what it actually costs

By Matt Geiss April 17, 2026 6 min read

Hot tubs are the weirdest thing in a junk-removal lineup. They weigh 2,500 to 4,500 pounds with water, they're hardwired to 220V electric, and most of them are too big to roll out the side yard in one piece. That's why this is its own job with its own price. Here's what it actually takes, what it usually costs in San Diego, and what makes the number move.

Starting cost range for San Diego

For a typical backyard hot tub (4 to 6 person, standard acrylic/fiberglass shell), removal runs $300 to $800 in the San Diego area. Most of the jobs we take land between $450 and $650.

That range covers a lot of different situations, which is why we always want a few photos and a couple of questions answered before quoting.

What drives the number

1. Access and path out

The single biggest variable. A hot tub on a flat side yard with a 4-foot gate that opens straight to the driveway is the cheap version. A hot tub on a second-story deck surrounded by a built-in gazebo, or in a backyard that's only reachable through a 30-inch side gate, is the expensive version.

Sometimes fence panels have to come down to get it out. That takes 15 minutes with a drill if the panels are screwed in, or an hour with a reciprocating saw if they're nailed and glued. We put the fence back when we're done.

2. Size

Two-person and spa-tub models come out easier and load faster. 8-person swim-spa and hot-tub-pool hybrids are a different category, sometimes requiring a second crew member or a heavier trailer. If your tub is bigger than a standard 7x7 footprint, mention that up front.

3. Whether it's drained and disconnected

Every hot tub needs to be drained before it moves. A typical spa holds 250 to 400 gallons. Drained by gravity through the bottom drain, that's 30 to 90 minutes depending on your slope. If you handle the drain before we arrive, the job is cheaper. We charge for the time if you haven't.

The electrical disconnect is the other pre-step. Most spas are on a dedicated 50 or 60-amp GFCI breaker, hardwired at a subpanel. If you're comfortable killing the breaker and disconnecting the whip at the subpanel, great. If not, we can do it, but we'll ask you to confirm the breaker is off before we touch it. We don't pull the whip at the house; that's an electrician's job if you want the disconnect cleaned up permanently.

4. Cover and surround

A loose vinyl cover is easy. A built-in cedar surround or a gazebo that the tub is inset into is a demolition job in itself. Those add $150 to $400 depending on what we're cutting through.

5. Disassembly

Most spas don't fit through a standard side gate in one piece. That means cutting with a sawzall on-site. We quarter the shell, pull the motor and plumbing, and bundle the pieces for the trailer. Disassembly adds 45 to 90 minutes depending on brand and how much foam insulation is wrapped around the plumbing.

The full process, start to finish

When we do a hot tub job, here's what the morning looks like:

  • Confirm the breaker is off and the tub is drained or draining.
  • Pop the side panels. Most brands have 4 to 8 screws holding access panels on. We check the plumbing and the motor area before cutting.
  • Disconnect the electrical whip at the tub side.
  • Cut the shell with a reciprocating saw. Usually into quarters or sixths. Foam goes everywhere, and we clean it up.
  • Pull the motor, pump, heater, and plumbing manifold. These go to scrap metal when we're done.
  • Load the pieces onto the trailer.
  • Re-install any fence panel or gate we removed.
  • Sweep. Hot tub demolition leaves a lot of foam and splinters. The pad or deck where the tub sat should look like the tub was never there.

Total time on site: 2 to 4 hours for a standard job. Half a day for bigger or more complex ones.

What happens to it after

The shell goes to the dump (these are typically non-recyclable acrylic over foam). The motor and pump go to scrap metal. Copper plumbing is separated and recycled. The cedar or composite surround, if any, is sorted for reuse or disposal. We don't pretend the whole thing gets recycled; most of the shell weight is headed to the landfill. But everything metal and everything reusable gets pulled out first.

What we need from you before quoting

Text us:

  • A photo of the tub from a few feet back
  • A photo showing the path out (gates, stairs, surround, any obstacles)
  • Approximate size (2-person, 4-person, 6-person, 8+)
  • Whether it's drained and whether the breaker has been off for at least 24 hours
  • Your zip code

We'll come back with a firm quote within the hour during business hours. If the situation is unusual (on a roof deck, needs a crane, built into a retaining wall), we come look in person before quoting.

Same-day hot tub removal

We do same-day jobs when the schedule allows. For hot tubs specifically, same-day works best when the tub is already drained, the breaker is off, and there's clear truck access to the backyard. If we're doing the drain and the electrical disconnect from scratch, that's almost always a next-day job.

One thing to watch for

If you're selling your house, a lot of real estate agents recommend removing an aging hot tub before listing. That's reasonable advice. What's less reasonable is waiting until the week before you list to get quotes. Give the project at least two weeks. The disposal pad underneath almost always needs a cleanup, a pressure wash, or a few new deck boards, and that's an extra trade to coordinate.
