How Junk Away We Go got started

Matt Geiss grew up watching his family work with their hands. That part of him never went away. After years in the private sector, a company-wide layoff left him with a decision: go looking for the next desk job, or do something he could run himself, out in the daylight, where the result of a day's work was actually visible at the end of it.

He bought one truck and a 12x8x5 dump trailer and started taking junk removal jobs around Poway. No franchise fee, no call center, no corporate script. Just a phone number, a tailgate, and a commitment that if he quoted a number, that was the number on the invoice.

That's still how Junk Away We Go runs today.

Matt built the business on a simple observation about the industry: customers hated the runaround. The big national brands wouldn't post pricing because they wanted to quote on-site, where it was harder to walk away. The Craigslist guys would quote a cheap number and then add hidden fees or dump half the load in a canyon. Neither was the business Matt wanted to run.

So he posted the prices on the website. Labor, dump fees, and taxes got rolled into one number. Surcharges got named up front instead of hidden in the fine print. Same-day premiums were quoted in the first text message, not sprung on the driveway. Over time, that transparency became the actual product.

What we do

Residential and commercial junk removal: garage cleanouts, estate cleanouts, appliance haul, e-waste, office de-commissioning, storage unit turnovers, tenant turnouts. Light demolition: sheds, decks, fences, hot tubs, interior walls, concrete slab breakout on smaller jobs. One crew, one truck, tear-out and haul-away in the same visit.

How we work

Matt reads every quote request personally. Text a photo, your zip code, and when you'd like it gone, and he'll come back with a load-tier estimate. For estate jobs, hoarder situations, and full construction tear-outs, he comes out to look in person before quoting, because guessing on those jobs costs somebody money.

We answer the phone 7 days of the week during business hours, and reply to texts in minutes, not hours. The truck shows up on time or we tell you why it's running late. We sweep after. On residential jobs, the driveway should look better than it did when we pulled up.

Where your stuff actually ends up

We sort every load before it hits the dump. Metal goes to the scrap yard. Electronics go to certified e-waste recyclers. Working appliances, reusable furniture, and unopened household goods get routed to local donation partners when there's a fit. It's more work than backing up to the dump and dropping everything, and it's the only reason we can call this an eco-friendly operation honestly.
